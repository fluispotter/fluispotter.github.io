Dried blood spot sampling

The device spots 10 µL blood samples onto a filter-paper strip, collecting up to 50 dried blood spots (DBS) per study for easy processing with standard punchers and analysis via HPLC, LC-MS/MS or ELISA. The non-heparin anticoagulant ensures consistent dilution without matrix effects — ideal for small molecules, peptides and biomarkers. Studies confirm DBS equivalence to whole blood, with high accuracy across hematocrit levels: Adhikari, K. B., et al. (2020); Krogh, J., et al. (2023).

The heparin-free system enhances assay compatibility and animal welfare, as demonstrated in minipig and canine models (Ollerenshaw, J. D., et al. (2022); Velschow, S., et al. (2016)). With a no-carry-over design and streamlined workflow, DBS sampling supports a wide range of compounds.

Publications

Peer-reviewed publications and abstracts highlighting the performance, safety and applications of the Fluispotter device.

  1. Adhikari, K. B., et al. "Fluispotter, a novel automated and wearable device for accurate volume serial dried blood spot sampling." Bioanalysis, vol. 12, no. 10, 2020, pp. 665–681.

    Evaluates the Fluispotter's sampling performance, demonstrating high accuracy and precision in serial dried blood spot sampling for cortisol analysis across a range of hematocrit values.

  2. Krogh, J., et al. "Validation of a novel automated system, Fluispotter®, for serial sampling of dried blood spots." Endocrine Connections, vol. 12, no. 7, 2023, e230087.

    Assesses the safety and performance of Fluispotter in healthy human subjects, confirming reliable cortisol profiles with minimal discomfort, while noting challenges with blood-flow clogging.

  3. Krogh, J., et al. "Serial sampling of dried blood spots collected by a novel automated body-worn system, Fluispotter®." Endocrine Abstracts, vol. 90, 2023, P439.

    Presented at the 25th European Congress of Endocrinology (ECE2023), detailing Fluispotter's application in automated serial sampling and its safety and acceptability in human subjects.

  4. Ollerenshaw, J. D., et al. "A novel device for serial venous blood sampling in a canine model." Journal of Pharmacological and Toxicological Methods, vol. 114, 2022, 107155.

    Demonstrates Fluispotter's performance in a canine model, showing no stress-related changes in blood chemistry or behavior — suitable for preclinical pharmacology and toxicology studies.

  5. Velschow, S., et al. "Technical and animal welfare perspectives of portable automated blood sampling with Fluispotter® in Göttingen Minipigs." Scandinavian Journal of Laboratory Animal Science, vol. 42, 2016.

    Explores the technical performance and welfare benefits of Fluispotter in Göttingen minipigs, emphasizing its stress-free sampling capabilities. (Published as a meeting abstract; contact the journal for access.)

About

Fluispotter was founded by Sten Velschow, an experienced laboratory-animal veterinarian and former Lundbeck executive with over 20 years in pharmaceutical research. The company was born from Sten's frustration with error-prone, inefficient blood sampling methods: running animal studies, he saw first-hand the stress, inconsistency and errors caused by manual techniques — compromising both animal welfare and data quality. In 2010, he launched Fluispotter to develop a device that automates blood sampling to reduce stress and improve data quality. Our team, with deep expertise in design, development and manufacturing of medical and consumer products, brings this vision to life.
