Dried blood spot sampling

The device spots 10 µL blood samples onto a filter-paper strip, collecting up to 50 dried blood spots (DBS) per study for easy processing with standard punchers and analysis via HPLC, LC-MS/MS or ELISA. The non-heparin anticoagulant ensures consistent dilution without matrix effects — ideal for small molecules, peptides and biomarkers. Studies confirm DBS equivalence to whole blood, with high accuracy across hematocrit levels: Adhikari et al. (2020); Krogh et al. (2023).

The heparin-free system enhances assay compatibility and animal welfare, as demonstrated in minipig and canine models (Ollerenshaw et al. (2022); Velschow et al. (2016)). With a no-carry-over design and streamlined workflow, DBS sampling supports a wide range of compounds.

Publications

Peer-reviewed publications and abstracts on the performance, safety and applications of the Fluispotter device.

  1. Adhikari, K. B., et al. "Fluispotter, a novel automated and wearable device for accurate volume serial dried blood spot sampling." Bioanalysis, vol. 12, no. 10, 2020, pp. 665–681.

    Demonstrates high accuracy and precision in serial dried blood spot sampling for cortisol analysis across a range of hematocrit values.

  2. Krogh, J., et al. "Validation of a novel automated system, Fluispotter®, for serial sampling of dried blood spots." Endocrine Connections, vol. 12, no. 7, 2023, e230087.

    Confirms reliable cortisol profiles in healthy human subjects with minimal discomfort, while noting challenges with blood-flow clogging.

  3. Krogh, J., et al. "Serial sampling of dried blood spots collected by a novel automated body-worn system, Fluispotter®." Endocrine Abstracts, vol. 90, 2023, P439.

    Presented at the 25th European Congress of Endocrinology (ECE2023); details automated serial sampling and its safety and acceptability in human subjects.

  4. Ollerenshaw, J. D., et al. "A novel device for serial venous blood sampling in a canine model." Journal of Pharmacological and Toxicological Methods, vol. 114, 2022, 107155.

    Shows no stress-related changes in blood chemistry or behavior in a canine model — suitable for preclinical pharmacology and toxicology.

  5. Velschow, S., et al. "Technical and animal welfare perspectives of portable automated blood sampling with Fluispotter® in Göttingen Minipigs." Scandinavian Journal of Laboratory Animal Science, vol. 42, 2016.

    Explores technical performance and welfare benefits in Göttingen minipigs. (Published as a meeting abstract; contact the journal for access.)

About

Fluispotter was founded in 2010 by Sten Velschow, an experienced laboratory-animal veterinarian and former Lundbeck executive with over 20 years in pharmaceutical research. The company was born from his frustration with error-prone, inefficient blood sampling: running animal studies, he saw first-hand the stress, inconsistency and errors of manual techniques — compromising both animal welfare and data quality. He set out to build a device that automates blood sampling to reduce stress and improve data quality. Our team, with deep expertise in the design, development and manufacturing of medical and consumer products, brings this vision to life.
